# Template: Testimonial and Social Proof Collection Guide

Use this guide when real testimonials are not yet available.
Do NOT write fake testimonials. Use this document to collect real ones.

---

## Why Testimonials Matter

Specific, attributed testimonials are among the highest-impact trust signals
on a landing page. A page with zero testimonials is more credible than a page
with invented ones.

Before publishing, prioritise collecting at least:
- 1 case study with measurable results
- 3 attributed testimonials with full name and company
- 1 result-focused testimonial that directly addresses the primary objection

---

## Customer Interview Questions

Ask these questions in a 15-minute call or email exchange. Use the exact
customer wording in testimonials — do not paraphrase into marketing language.

### Before

1. What were you struggling with before you found us?
2. How long had this been a problem for you?
3. What had you already tried to fix it?
4. What was the cost of that problem — in time, money, or missed opportunity?

### Decision

5. What made you decide to reach out to us?
6. Was there anything that made you hesitant before getting started?
7. What made you decide to go ahead despite that hesitation?

### Experience

8. What was the process like once you started working with us?
9. Was there anything that surprised you (positively or negatively)?

### Results

10. What has specifically changed since you started working with us?
11. Can you give me a specific result — a number, a percentage, a time frame?
12. What does your situation look like now compared to before?

### Recommendation

13. Who would you recommend this to?
14. What would you tell a friend or colleague who is in the same situation you were in?

## Testimonial Format — Short Form

Use this format for quote cards or trust strips.

```
"[1–2 sentence direct quote using their exact words]"

— [Full Name], [Job Title], [Company Name]
```

**Good example:**
> "We had tried three different agencies before. Within six weeks of working
> with them, we had a brand we were actually proud to show potential clients."
>
> — Sarah L., Founder, Meridian Consulting

**Poor example:**
> "Great service! Very professional. Would recommend."
>
> — A Happy Customer

The second example lacks specificity, attribution, and a result. Do not use
testimonials like this without pressing for more detail.

---

## Testimonial Format — Full Story (Before / After / Bridge)

Use this format for case study sections and extended social proof.

```
BEFORE
[1–2 sentences: situation before. What were they struggling with?]

PROBLEM
[1–2 sentences: the specific problem and its cost]

DECISION
[1 sentence: why they chose this product/service]

EXPERIENCE
[1–2 sentences: what it was like to work together]

RESULT
[1–2 sentences: the specific, measurable outcome]

TRANSFORMATION
[1 sentence: how things are different now]

QUOTE
"[Their words, unedited]"
— [Full Name], [Job Title], [Company Name]
```

## Case Study Structure

For high-value or complex offers, a structured case study carries more weight
than a short testimonial.

```
CLIENT BACKGROUND
Who they are, their industry, their team size.

CHALLENGE
What specific problem brought them to you.
What they had tried before. Why it hadn't worked.

SOLUTION
What you did. How you approached it.
Key decisions made. Why this approach was right for them.

RESULTS
Specific, verifiable outcomes. Numbers, percentages, timeframes.
[PLACEHOLDER — insert real metrics with client permission]

CLIENT QUOTE
"[Direct quote in their words]"
— [Full Name], [Job Title], [Company Name]

WHAT MADE THE DIFFERENCE
One sentence on the key factor that drove the result.
```

## Consent and Usage Rights

Before using any testimonial or case study:

- [ ] Get written consent from the customer
- [ ] Confirm the exact quote is approved for use
- [ ] Confirm the customer's name, title, and company can be published
- [ ] Confirm permission to use their photo (if including headshots)
- [ ] Confirm permission to name their company (some clients request anonymity)

**Template consent message:**

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you so much for the kind words. We'd love to share your experience
> on our website and marketing materials. Would you be comfortable with us
> using the following quote with your name and company?
>
> "[Quote]"
> — [Their name], [Their title], [Company]
>
> If you'd prefer to adjust the wording, or if you'd rather remain anonymous,
> just let us know and we'll work with whatever you're comfortable sharing.

## Review Platform Integration

If the business has reviews on public platforms, link to them rather than
copying them without permission.

Recommended platforms to reference (with permission):
- Google Business reviews
- Clutch.co (for agencies and service providers)
- G2 / Capterra (for SaaS)
- Trustpilot
- App Store / Google Play (for mobile apps)

Always verify that platform terms of service allow embedding or quoting
reviews before doing so.
