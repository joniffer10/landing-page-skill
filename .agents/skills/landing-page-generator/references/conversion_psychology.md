# Conversion Psychology Reference

Core psychological principles that underpin high-converting landing pages.
Apply these when making structural and copy decisions.

---

## AIDA Framework

The foundational model for moving a visitor to action.

| Stage | What happens | Section where this applies |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | The visitor notices the page | Hero headline |
| Interest | The visitor wants to know more | Problem + Solution |
| Desire | The visitor wants the product | Benefits + Reinforcement |
| Action | The visitor converts | CTA sections |

**Practical rule:** Every section must advance the visitor from one stage to
the next. A section that stalls progress should be removed or restructured.

---

## PAS: Problem-Agitate-Solution

The most reliable structure for persuasive body copy.

```
Problem    → Name the problem the visitor already recognises
Agitate    → Deepen the emotional weight of the problem
Solution   → Present the product as the specific, credible answer
```

Use PAS for:
- The Problem section
- Individual benefit explanations
- Email follow-up sequences
- Short-form ad copy

**Example:**

> Running a business without a clear brand is exhausting. Every time a
> potential client looks you up, you wonder if your website is costing you
> the job. Meanwhile, competitors who look more professional are winning
> projects you are more qualified to deliver. There is a faster way to
> fix this — and it does not require starting from scratch.

## Loss Aversion

People are roughly twice as motivated by the prospect of losing something
than by the prospect of gaining the same thing (Kahneman & Tversky).

**How to apply:**
- Frame inaction as a cost: "Every month without a clear value proposition
  is another month of lost leads."
- Use contrast: show the before state (painful) next to the after state
  (desired) to make the gap feel real.
- In the Offer section: frame the guarantee as eliminating risk, not adding
  a bonus.

**Prohibited application:**
- Do not manufacture fear. Loss aversion is only effective when the cost of
  inaction is real and verifiable.
- Do not use dark patterns (countdown timers that reset, false scarcity).

## Social Proof

People follow the actions of others, especially in conditions of uncertainty
(Cialdini, Influence).

Types of social proof, in order of conversion impact:

| Type | Impact | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Specific case study with named client + metrics | Highest | Requires permission |
| Named testimonial with photo and company | Very high | Get written consent |
| Named testimonial without photo | High | Still effective |
| Review count + rating (e.g. 4.8 from 212 reviews) | Medium-high | Requires real data |
| Unnamed testimonial | Medium | Reduced credibility |
| "Trusted by X customers" | Medium | Only if the number is real |
| Press logos | Medium | Requires real press coverage |
| Client logo bar | Low-medium | Requires permission from each client |

**Rule:** Never fabricate any social proof. A page with zero social proof is
more credible than a page with invented social proof.

When no proof is available, use `[PLACEHOLDER]` and provide the testimonial
guide (`templates/testimonial_guide.md`) to collect real proof.

## Cognitive Load

The mental effort required to process information. High cognitive load reduces
conversion.

**How to reduce cognitive load:**
- One primary CTA per page (multiple competing CTAs create paralysis)
- Clear visual hierarchy (large headline, readable body, obvious button)
- Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences)
- Scannable structure (headlines, subheadings, bullets for lists)
- Progressive disclosure (lead with the most important information)
- Consistent terminology (do not call the product different names in different sections)

**Signs of high cognitive load in a landing page:**
- Multiple equally prominent CTAs
- Dense, unbroken paragraphs
- Jargon without explanation
- Features described without context
- Navigation links that lead visitors away from the page

## Anchoring

The first number or price a visitor sees influences how they evaluate
subsequent numbers.

**How to apply:**
- In a pricing section, show the full value (or the cost of the problem)
  before showing the price
- When offering tiered pricing, show the middle or recommended tier first
- When using a "was/now" discount, only use it if the original price is real
  and the discount is genuine

---

## Reciprocity

People feel obligated to return a favour (Cialdini, Influence).

**How to apply:**
- Offer genuine value before asking for commitment: a free guide, a free
  consultation, a free trial, a free audit
- The more specific and useful the free offer, the stronger the reciprocity response
- Do not use reciprocity as a manipulation device — it only works when the
  free value is genuinely useful

---

## Authority and Credibility

People defer to credible experts, especially for high-commitment decisions.

**How to establish authority:**
- Specific credentials (relevant certifications, press, speaking engagements)
- Demonstrated expertise (case studies, methodology, track record)
- Founder/team story that shows relevant experience
- Third-party validation (awards, accreditations — real only)

**Signs of false authority (avoid):**
- "Industry-leading" without any evidence
- "Years of combined experience" (often a vague substitute for real credentials)
- Unnamed "expert" endorsements
- Stock photography presented as the real team

---

## Commitment and Consistency

Once someone makes a small commitment, they are more likely to make a
larger commitment later (Cialdini, Influence).

**How to apply:**
- Use a secondary CTA that is lower-commitment than the primary CTA
  (e.g. "Download the free guide" before "Book a call")
- Structure the form to ask for low-risk information first (name, email)
  before higher-risk information (budget, company size)
- Use micro-commitments in copy: "If you've read this far, you already know
  the problem is real."

---

## The Paradox of Choice

Too many options reduce the likelihood of any decision being made (Schwartz).

**How to apply:**
- Offer one primary CTA on the page
- If pricing tiers exist, offer a maximum of 3, with a recommended option
  highlighted
- Do not give the visitor a menu of services when the goal is a single conversion
- In FAQ, do not introduce new options or complexity — only resolve concerns

---

## Friction and Conversion

Every unnecessary step between intention and action reduces conversion.

**Sources of friction on a landing page:**
- Long or complex forms
- Slow page load
- Confusing navigation
- Missing trust signals
- Unanswered objections
- Unclear next steps after clicking CTA
- Requiring account creation before demonstrating value

**Rule:** Before the final CTA, identify every remaining friction point and
address it — either by removing it or by pre-answering it in the FAQ or
How It Works section.
