# Copywriting Formulas Reference

Structural frameworks for organising persuasive copy. Each formula is suited
to different sections and conversion contexts.

---

## Formula Selection Guide

| Formula | Best for | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|
| FAB | Benefits section, feature explanations | High-emotion contexts — too logical |
| PAS | Problem section, ad copy, email | Product is already desired — skip agitation |
| BAB | Reinforcement, hero sub-copy | Audience already aware of problem |
| PASTOR | Long-form sales pages, complex offers | Short-form squeeze pages |
| 4Ps | Hero sections, email subject lines | When proof is not yet available |
| 4Cs | All sections — universal quality filter | Never ignore this checklist |
| AIDA | Full page structure macro | N/A — use as overarching frame |

---

## FAB — Feature, Advantage, Benefit

Connects what a product does to why it matters to the customer.

```
Feature     → What it is or does
Advantage   → How it is better than the alternative
Benefit     → What the customer actually gains
```

**Template:**

> [Product/feature] [does/has X], which means [advantage vs. alternative],
> so you [specific benefit].

**Example:**

> Our drag-and-drop website editor lets you make changes without touching code,
> which means you're not waiting on a developer every time you want to update
> a price or swap a photo, so you stay in control of your own business.

**Where to use:** Section 3.5 Benefits (each benefit block), product page copy,
feature comparison tables.

---

## PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

The most reliable framework for connecting emotionally before presenting a solution.

```
Problem     → Name the problem the reader already recognises
Agitate     → Deepen the emotional weight: consequence, frustration, cost
Solution    → Present the product as the specific, credible answer
```

**Template:**

> [Name the problem they recognise].
> [Deepen the consequence — what this is actually costing them].
> [Introduce the solution as the specific answer].

**Example:**

> Most service businesses have a website that hasn't been updated in years.
> The problem isn't the design — it's that every time a potential client visits,
> the outdated copy, the missing portfolio, the broken contact form is doing the
> selling. And it's losing the sale before you ever get on a call.
> There's a better way to approach this.

**Where to use:** Section 3.3 Problem, individual benefit copy, email sequences,
ad copy.

---

## BAB — Before, After, Bridge

Focuses on transformation. Shows where the customer is and where they could be.

```
Before   → Current painful state the reader recognises
After    → Desired future state they want
Bridge   → The product as the path between the two
```

**Template:**

> [Right now, you are dealing with X].
> [Imagine instead: Y].
> [Product] is how you get there.

**Example:**

> Right now, every new project starts the same way — you quote, you wait,
> you follow up, you lose half of them to a competitor with a slicker pitch.
> Imagine opening your inbox to booked consultations instead of chased leads.
> That is what a properly positioned brand and website makes possible.

**Where to use:** Section 3.8 Reinforcement, hero subheadings, closing copy,
email follow-up sequences.

---

## PASTOR — Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response

A longer-form framework for high-commitment offers where trust must be built
before the sale.

```
Problem         → Name the core problem
Amplify         → Show the consequences and emotional cost
Story           → A case study, client story, or founder story that proves the solution
Transformation  → Show the after state — what becomes possible
Offer           → Present the specific offer
Response        → The CTA — what they do next
```

**Where to use:** Long-form sales pages, premium service pages, high-ticket
offers where the visitor needs substantial proof before committing.

**Note:** Do not use invented stories. If no real case study exists, use
`[PLACEHOLDER — insert real client story here]` and mark it as needed.

## 4Ps — Promise, Picture, Proof, Push

A concise framework effective for hero sections and email subject lines.

```
Promise   → Make a bold, specific promise
Picture   → Paint a vivid picture of the outcome
Proof     → Provide evidence the promise is credible
Push      → Ask for the specific action
```

**Example:**

> Promise: A brand that wins clients before you open your mouth.
> Picture: Imagine a prospect landing on your website and already feeling
>          confident in your expertise before they read a single word.
> Proof:   [PLACEHOLDER — insert client result or case study]
> Push:    Book your brand strategy session.

**Where to use:** Hero sections, email subject lines, short-form ad copy.

## 4Cs — Clear, Concise, Compelling, Credible

A universal quality filter. Apply to every piece of copy before submitting.

| C | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Clear | Does a first-time visitor immediately understand what this is and who it's for? |
| Concise | Can any word be removed without losing meaning? |
| Compelling | Does the copy motivate the reader to keep reading or take action? |
| Credible | Is every claim supported by evidence or clearly marked as an assumption? |

**Rule:** If a piece of copy fails any one of these tests, revise it before
including it in the output.

## The So What? Test

Apply to every benefit claim. Ask "so what?" until the answer is an outcome
the customer cares about emotionally.

**Example chain:**

> "Our platform integrates with your CRM."
> So what?
> "So you don't have to manually copy data between systems."
> So what?
> "So your sales team spends time selling instead of doing admin."
> So what?
> "So you close more deals without hiring more people."
> ← This is the outcome worth writing about.

Stop at the first answer the customer would recognise as personally meaningful.

---

## The Because Test

Claims become more persuasive when followed by "because" (Langer, 1978).

**Weak:** "Our process saves you time."
**Strong:** "Our process saves you time because every deliverable is built to
a clear brief — no rounds of guesswork, no starting over."

Apply "because" to every major claim in the benefits and differentiation sections.

---

## Contrast Principle

Readers understand value better when they can compare it to a reference point.

**Types of contrast:**
- Before vs. After: current painful state vs. desired future state
- Us vs. Alternative: your approach vs. what they've probably tried
- Cost of problem vs. cost of solution: what inaction is costing them
- Time with product vs. time without: how much faster things get

**Rule:** Every major value claim is stronger when paired with its contrast.
