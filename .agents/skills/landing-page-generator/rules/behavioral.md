# Behavioral Rules

These are hard constraints. They apply to every landing page generated by this
skill, without exception.

---

## Absolute Prohibitions

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | Never fabricate business facts (founding year, team size, locations, revenue) |
| 2 | Never fabricate testimonials, quotes, or customer stories |
| 3 | Never fabricate statistics or percentages |
| 4 | Never fabricate awards, certifications, or press coverage |
| 5 | Never fabricate client names, logos, or case study results |
| 6 | Never manufacture urgency that is not verified as real |
| 7 | Never manufacture scarcity (e.g. "Only 3 spots left!") unless confirmed |
| 8 | Never attack a named competitor or make unsupported superiority claims |
| 9 | Never guarantee outcomes the business cannot control |
| 10 | Never reproduce distinctive phrasing from any living copywriter |

Violating any of these rules in output is a critical failure. Do not proceed.

---

## [PLACEHOLDER] Tagging Protocol

When a required fact is missing from the inputs, insert a placeholder using
this exact format:

```
[PLACEHOLDER — brief description of what is needed]
```

Examples:

```
[PLACEHOLDER — client logo: get written permission before use]
[PLACEHOLDER — founding year]
[PLACEHOLDER — money-back guarantee details]
[PLACEHOLDER — case study result: specific metric from client]
```

Rules for placeholders:
- Always use square brackets
- Always include a brief description of what is needed
- Never leave a placeholder vague (e.g. [PLACEHOLDER] alone is insufficient)
- List every placeholder in the Placeholder Log (templates/placeholder_log.md)

---

## [ASSUMPTION] Tagging Protocol

When the AI makes a reasonable strategic decision in the absence of input,
tag it clearly:

```
[ASSUMPTION — brief description]
```

Examples:

```
[ASSUMPTION — tone is professional and warm; confirm with client]
[ASSUMPTION — target audience is English-speaking; verify geography]
[ASSUMPTION — no existing guarantee; recommend adding one]
```

Rules for assumptions:
- Assumptions are acceptable for strategic decisions (tone, structure, framing)
- Assumptions are never acceptable as substitutes for facts
- Always distinguish assumptions from verified information in the output
- List every assumption in the Placeholder Log

## Urgency and Scarcity Rules

Real urgency is acceptable when it is:
- Tied to a genuine deadline (e.g. early-bird pricing that expires on a real date)
- Tied to genuine inventory or capacity limits confirmed by the client
- Expressed honestly and specifically

Prohibited urgency phrases (unless verified):
- "Only X spots left!"
- "Offer expires soon"
- "Limited time only" (without a real date)
- "Don't miss out" (without a genuine reason)
- "Hurry before it's too late"

When urgency cannot be verified, omit it entirely rather than manufacture it.

---

## Competitive Comparison Rules

Permitted:
- Comparing categories of solution (e.g. DIY vs. professional service)
- Naming general alternative approaches (e.g. "hiring a freelancer")
- Describing common trade-offs customers face

Not permitted:
- Naming specific competitor brands in a negative framing
- Claiming specific superiority over a named competitor without evidence
- Using a competitor's trademarked name in a way that implies endorsement

---

## Structure Adaptation Rules

The default 13-section page structure is a starting point, not a constraint.

Adapt the structure when:
- The business model requires it (e.g. SaaS vs. service vs. physical product)
- The conversion goal is low-friction (e.g. free trial) vs. high-friction (e.g. custom enterprise quote)
- The audience is highly informed and does not need extensive education
- The page is a short-form squeeze page rather than a full sales page

Every section included must have a clear strategic reason to exist.  
Every section excluded must be consciously removed, not accidentally omitted.

---

## Output Quality Standards

- Do not pad output to increase length
- Do not produce trivial variations (every variation must offer a different angle)
- Do not include sections that add no conversion value
- Do not present the company as the hero — the customer is always the hero
- Do not end the document without running the validators internally
