# Copywriting Rules

All copy produced by this skill must follow these rules without exception.
For formulas and frameworks, see `references/copywriting_formulas.md`.
For psychological principles, see `references/conversion_psychology.md`.

---

## Core Principle

> **Features explain. Benefits persuade. Outcomes sell.**

Always apply this hierarchy:

```
Feature    → what the product does or has
Benefit    → why that matters to the customer
Outcome    → what the customer's life looks like after
```

Never stop at the feature. Always reach the outcome.

---

## Voice and Tone

| Attribute | Rule |
|---|---|
| Person | Write in second person: "you", "your" |
| Subject | The customer is the subject; the product is the tool |
| Active voice | Prefer "You get results" over "Results are delivered" |
| Sentence length | Mix short punchy sentences with medium explanatory ones |
| Paragraph length | 2–4 sentences maximum per paragraph in body copy |
| Reading level | Target Grade 8–10 unless the audience requires technical language |
| Tone default | Direct, warm, and specific |

Tone adapts to the brand brief. If no brief is provided, use the default above.

---

## The Do List

- Write directly to the reader
- Name the specific problem before offering the solution
- Use contrast: before state vs. after state
- Use specificity: "3 hours" not "a long time"
- Use verbs that show action: "build", "launch", "attract", "eliminate"
- Open each section with a hook that earns the next sentence
- Close each section with a transition that earns the next section
- Put the most important word at the start or end of a sentence, not buried in the middle
- Match the emotional state of the reader at each stage of the page
- Use "because" when making claims — it triggers automatic compliance

---

## The Do Not List

| Prohibited | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Corporate jargon (leverage, synergize, optimize, scalable) | Plain verbs: use, improve, grow |
| Generic AI phrases (game-changing, revolutionary, seamlessly) | Specific, measurable outcomes |
| Excessive exclamation marks | Period. Let the copy carry the energy. |
| Vague superlatives (best, fastest, easiest) | Specific proof or qualified claims |
| Passive constructions | Active verbs |
| "We are passionate about" | Show it through action and results |
| "Solutions" as a noun | Name what the product actually does |
| "World-class" / "industry-leading" | Prove it with a specific credential |
| Long walls of unbroken text | Break into short paragraphs or bullet blocks |
| Repetition across sections | Each section must introduce something new |

## Prohibited Phrases List

Never use these phrases in any output:

- "Transform your business"
- "Unlock your potential"
- "Cutting-edge"
- "State-of-the-art"
- "Next-level"
- "Seamlessly integrates"
- "Robust solution"
- "End-to-end"
- "Holistic approach"
- "Synergize"
- "Leverage" (as a verb for "use")
- "Pain points" (in copy — acceptable in strategy notes)
- "Move the needle"
- "Best-in-class"
- "Thought leader"
- "Ecosystem"
- "Disruptive"
- "Empower" (overused — replace with specific verbs)
- "Reimagine"
- "Transformative"

## Headline Writing Rules

Headlines must answer at least one of:
- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- What outcome does it deliver?
- Why should I care right now?

**Good headline patterns:**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Specific outcome | "Get More Qualified Leads Without Spending More on Ads" |
| Problem reversal | "Stop Losing Clients to Competitors Who Look More Professional" |
| Time frame | "Launch a High-Converting Website in Under 4 Weeks" |
| For + who + outcome | "For Funded Startups That Need a Brand That Matches Their Ambition" |
| Contrast | "Your Competitors Have a System. Here's How to Build Yours." |
| Question | "What If Every Page of Your Website Actually Converted?" |

**Avoid:**
- "Transform Your Business Today" — vague, no audience, no outcome
- "Welcome to [Brand]" — wastes the hero's most valuable real estate
- "The Future of [Industry] Is Here" — empty hype
- Headlines over 15 words — unless deliberately structured as a two-liner

## CTA Writing Rules

Every CTA must:
1. Start with a strong action verb
2. Communicate what happens next (the immediate next step)
3. Reinforce the benefit where possible without becoming a sentence
4. Reduce uncertainty or anxiety
5. Be specific to the conversion context

**Strong action verbs:**
Start, Get, Build, Book, Launch, Claim, See, Try, Join, Discover, Create, Send

**Strong CTA patterns:**

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Verb + next step | "Book My Free Consultation" |
| Verb + benefit | "Get My Growth Plan" |
| Verb + outcome | "Start Attracting Better Clients" |
| Verb + low-risk signal | "Try It Free for 14 Days" |
| Verb + personalisation | "Build My Custom Quote" |

**Weak CTAs to replace:**
- "Submit" → "Send My Request"
- "Click Here" → describe the destination
- "Learn More" → "See How It Works"
- "Get Started" → be specific about what starting means
- "Buy Now" → only use when the purchase is the obvious and only next step

## Body Copy Structure

For each section, use this structure:

```
1. Hook sentence   — earn the reader's attention
2. Problem/tension — name what they recognise
3. Bridge         — connect tension to the solution
4. Proof/support  — back up the claim
5. Outcome        — show the desired future state
6. Transition     — lead naturally to the next section
```

Not every element is required in every section. Use judgment.

---

## Storytelling Framework

When using narrative in any section (especially Problem and About):

```
Situation   → Set the scene the reader recognises
Conflict    → Name the problem or tension
Consequence → Show the cost of the current situation
Resolution  → Introduce the turning point
Outcome     → Describe the transformation
```

Keep stories relevant and brief. One storytelling angle per section maximum.

---

## Objection Handling Rules

Every major objection must be addressed before the final CTA. Address them:
- Implicitly through proof (testimonials, case studies, metrics)
- Directly in FAQ answers
- Through risk reversal in the offer section (guarantee, free trial)
- Through the How It Works section (reducing process anxiety)

Never address an objection by dismissing it.  
Always acknowledge the concern before reframing it.

Example:

> Weak: "Price is not an issue because we offer great value."
> Strong: "Most clients tell us they hesitated on cost. After the first project,
> the question they wish they'd asked sooner is: what was the cost of waiting?"
